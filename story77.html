Former ICC Founding Judge Georghios M. Pikis Passes Away

Published: February 3, 2026

Announcement: International Criminal Court statement issued February 3, 2026

Breaking: The International Criminal Court announced today that Georghios M. Pikis, one of the Court's founding judges and a Cypriot national instrumental in establishing its early jurisprudence, passed away on Thursday, January 29, 2026.

Key Facts

  • Georghios M. Pikis was among the first 18 judges elected to the ICC during its inaugural election in February 2003 and served a six-year term through 2009.
  • During his tenure, Pikis sat in the Appeals Division, including service as its President, shaping precedent across the Court's formative years.
  • Before joining the ICC, he held senior judicial roles in Cyprus, serving on the Supreme Court from 1981 and leading it as President from 1995 to 2004.
  • Pikis previously acted as an ad hoc judge at the European Court of Human Rights in 1993 and 1997 and was a member of the United Nations Committee against Torture from 1996 to 1998.
  • His career also included chairing the XIIIth Conference of European Constitutional Courts, underscoring his influence across regional legal systems.

Institutional Impact

Serving on the ICC from its earliest days, Pikis helped interpret the Rome Statute's foundational provisions, laying groundwork for the Court's procedural and substantive frameworks. Colleagues credit him with bridging civil and common law traditions within the Appeals Division and reinforcing protections for defendants' rights during the Court's nascent phase.

Tributes from ICC Leadership

ICC President Tomoko Akane: "His passing is an enormous loss for the ICC and international justice. Judge Pikis's passion, dedication, and sincerity - along with his indispensable contribution to the Court's institutional framework and jurisprudence - will continue to guide our work."
Assembly of States Parties President Paivi Kaukoranta: "We honor Judge Pikis for his contribution to the Rome Statute system and his steadfast commitment to fairness and due process."

Condolence Information

The ICC will open a condolence book in the Court's lobby from February 4 through February 9, 2026. Written condolences may be shared via email at Condolence@icc-cpi.int.
