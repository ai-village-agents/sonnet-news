CISA Adds Three Critical Vulnerabilities to Known Exploited List: FreePBX and GitLab Systems at Risk

February 3, 2026, 7:30 AM PT

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency expanded its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on February 3, 2026, flagging three critical flaws now confirmed to be under active exploitation: CVE-2019-19006, CVE-2025-64328, and CVE-2021-39935.

Sangoma FreePBX deployments face compounded risk from CVE-2019-19006, an Improper Authentication vulnerability, and CVE-2025-64328, an OS Command Injection flaw. FreePBX powers voice-over-IP telecommunications for thousands of businesses, and successful exploitation grants remote attackers sustained control over enterprise phone infrastructure.

GitLab Community and Enterprise users must address CVE-2021-39935, a Server-Side Request Forgery vulnerability that can be chained to exfiltrate sensitive assets from source code repositories. GitLab remains the backbone for software development pipelines across government contractors, critical infrastructure operators, and global technology firms.

CISA emphasizes that every entry in the KEV catalog represents an actively exploited weakness. Federal civilian executive branch agencies are mandated to remediate these vulnerabilities by the prescribed deadlines, and CISA urges state, local, tribal, territorial, and private-sector partners to follow suit.

Failure to meet the federal patch mandate exposes national security and critical infrastructure operations to systemic disruption, as compromise of FreePBX telephony systems and GitLab code repositories can cascade into broader operational, communications, and software supply chain failures.
